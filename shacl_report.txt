2. Comparison: OWL Reasoner vs. SHACL (Week 5, Session B):
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- One check an OWL Reasoner can do that SHACL cannot:
  Logical Consistency and Unsatisfiability under Open World Assumption (OWA).
  An OWL reasoner (e.g. HermiT or Pellet) performs semantic inferencing. For
  example, if :worksAt has rdfs:range :Organization, and :Place is declared
  owl:disjointWith :Organization, an OWL reasoner infers that any entity
  asserted as an object of :worksAt is an :Organization. If another axiom
  asserts or infers that the same entity is a :Place, the reasoner deduces that
  the entity belongs to the empty class (:Place ⊓ :Organization ⊑ ⊥) and proves
  the entire ontology logically inconsistent. SHACL does not perform open-world
  subsumption reasoning or automated classification across complex OWL class
  expressions unless explicit RDF type triples exist.

- One check SHACL can do that an OWL Reasoner cannot:
  Closed-World Cardinality and Lexical Constraints (Closed World Assumption, CWA).
  Under the Open World Assumption (OWA) of OWL, if :Hans_Bethe has two distinct
  birthplaces (:Strasbourg and :Germany) and :bornIn is an owl:FunctionalProperty,
  an OWL reasoner does NOT report a violation. Instead, it infers that :Strasbourg
  owl:sameAs :Germany! If no explicit disjointness between the two individuals is
  stated, the reasoner simply assumes they are two names for the same entity.
  An OWL reasoner cannot count known triples and state "you have 2 assertions in
  your file, which exceeds the limit of 1". In contrast, SHACL operates under
  the Closed World Assumption: it inspects the exact graph data, counts the two
  distinct edges, detects that 2 > 1, and raises a constraint violation. SHACL can
  also enforce string patterns, regexes, and value length checks that fall
  outside the scope of OWL logic.
